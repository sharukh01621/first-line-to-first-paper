Appendix B — Essential Mathematics, Biostatistics & Deep Learning Equations Refresher

How to Use This Appendix: Every equation in this reference card was derived from first principles and verified numerically across Chapters 16–36. Each section pairs concise mathematical formulas with geometric or clinical intuition and cross-references to the exact chapters where you built them in NumPy, SciPy, Scikit-Learn, or PyTorch.

B.1 Linear Algebra & Matrix Calculus (Chapters 16, 22, 25, 27)

B.1.1 Dot Products, Vector Norms & Cosine Similarity

For vectors \(u, v \in \mathbb{R}^p\):

\[u \cdot v = u^\top v = \sum_{j=1}^{p} u_j v_j = \|u\|_2 \|v\|_2 \cos\theta\]

\[\cos\theta = \frac{u^\top v}{\|u\|_2 \|v\|_2}, \quad \|v\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^{p} |v_j|, \quad \|v\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{p} v_j^2}, \quad \|v\|_\infty = \max_j |v_j|\]

  • Intuition: \(\|v\|_1\) penalizes total absolute magnitude and promotes exact sparsity (Lasso); \(\|v\|_2\) measures Euclidean length and shrinks weights smoothly (Ridge, weight decay); \(\cos\theta\) measures directional alignment independent of magnitude (embedding retrieval and attention).

B.1.2 Matrix Multiplication Shape Rule & Frobenius Norm

For \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times k}\), the product \(C = AB \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) requires matching inner dimensions:

\[(n, p) \times (p, k) \longrightarrow (n, k), \qquad C_{ij} = \sum_{m=1}^{p} A_{im} B_{mj}\]

\[\|A\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{p} A_{ij}^2} = \sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(A^\top A)}\]

B.1.3 Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) & PCA Variance-MSE Identity

Let \(X_c \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}\) be a column-centered data matrix (\(\bar{x}_j = 0\)). Its thin SVD factors \(X_c\) into orthonormal left singular vectors \(U \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}\), non-negative singular values \(\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r)\) with \(\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r \ge 0\), and orthonormal right singular vectors (principal axes) \(V \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times r}\):

\[X_c = U \Sigma V^\top, \qquad S = \frac{1}{n - 1} X_c^\top X_c = V \left(\frac{\Sigma^2}{n - 1}\right) V^\top\]

The PCA Variance-MSE Identity (Chapter 25) proves that projecting onto the top \(k\) right singular vectors \(V_k \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times k}\) simultaneously maximizes retained variance and minimizes orthogonal reconstruction error:

\[\underbrace{\frac{1}{n} \|X_c\|_F^2}_{\text{Total variance}} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{n} \|X_c V_k\|_F^2}_{\text{Projected variance}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{n} \|X_c - X_c V_k V_k^\top\|_F^2}_{\text{Reconstruction MSE}}, \qquad \text{EVR}_j = \frac{\sigma_j^2}{\sum_{m=1}^{r} \sigma_m^2}\]

B.1.4 Essential Matrix Calculus Identities

For constant vector \(a \in \mathbb{R}^p\) and square matrix \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}\):

\[\nabla_w (a^\top w) = a, \qquad \nabla_w (w^\top A w) = (A + A^\top) w, \qquad \nabla_w \|Xw - y\|_2^2 = 2 X^\top (Xw - y)\]

B.2 Probability, Distributions & Epidemiological Metrics (Chapters 20, 23, 34)

B.2.1 Bayes’ Theorem (Diagnostic & Clinical Screening Form)

For disease event \(D\) with prior prevalence \(P(D)\) and positive test \(T^+\):

\[P(D \mid T^+) = \frac{P(T^+ \mid D) P(D)}{P(T^+ \mid D) P(D) + P(T^+ \mid D^c) \bigl(1 - P(D)\bigr)}\]

  • Clinical Intuition (Base-Rate Neglect): Even a test with \(95\%\) sensitivity and \(95\%\) specificity yields a positive predictive value (PPV) under \(17\%\) when true disease prevalence is \(1\%\). Always report prevalence alongside PPV and PR-AUC.

B.2.2 Wilson Score 95% Confidence Interval for a Proportion

For \(k\) events in \(n\) trials with sample proportion \(\hat{p} = k / n\) and \(z = z_{1 - \alpha/2}\) (\(z \approx 1.96\) for \(95\%\) CI):

\[\text{CI}_{\text{Wilson}} = \frac{\hat{p} + \frac{z^2}{2n} \pm z \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n} + \frac{z^2}{4n^2}}}{1 + \frac{z^2}{n}}\]

  • Why Wilson Beats Wald: Unlike the naive Wald interval \(\hat{p} \pm z\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n}\), the Wilson interval never produces impossible probabilities below \(0\) or above \(1\), and maintains reliable coverage even when \(k = 0\) or \(n\) is small.

B.2.3 \(2 \times 2\) Epidemiological Table: Relative Risk & Odds Ratio (Woolf CI)

Let a \(2 \times 2\) exposure-outcome table contain exposed cases (\(a\)), exposed non-cases (\(b\)), unexposed cases (\(c\)), and unexposed non-cases (\(d\)):

\[\text{RR} = \frac{a / (a + b)}{c / (c + d)}, \qquad \text{OR} = \frac{a / b}{c / d} = \frac{a d}{b c}\]

Because \(\text{OR}\) is right-skewed, compute its Woolf 95% CI on the log scale and exponentiate back:

\[\text{SE}(\ln \text{OR}) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c} + \frac{1}{d}}, \qquad \text{CI}_{95\%}(\text{OR}) = \exp\!\Bigl(\ln \text{OR} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}(\ln \text{OR})\Bigr)\]

B.3 Hypothesis Testing & Multiple Comparisons (Chapters 20, 33, 34)

B.3.1 Parametric & Non-Parametric Two-Group and Multi-Group Tests

Welch’s Unequal-Variance \(t\)-Test (default over Student’s \(t\)-test in Chapter 20):

\[t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}, \qquad \nu \approx \frac{\left(\dfrac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \dfrac{s_2^2}{n_2}\right)^2}{\dfrac{(s_1^2 / n_1)^2}{n_1 - 1} + \dfrac{(s_2^2 / n_2)^2}{n_2 - 1}}\]

Cohen’s \(d\) Standardized Effect Size (always report alongside \(p\)-values):

\[d = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_{\text{pooled}}}, \qquad s_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\frac{(n_1 - 1) s_1^2 + (n_2 - 1) s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}}\]

Mann-Whitney \(U\) Statistic (rank-sum alternative for skewed biomarkers):

\[U_1 = R_1 - \frac{n_1 (n_1 + 1)}{2}, \qquad U = \min(U_1, n_1 n_2 - U_1)\]

Pearson’s Chi-Square Test of Independence (\(r \times c\) contingency table):

\[\chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{c} \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}, \qquad E_{ij} = \frac{R_i C_j}{N}, \qquad df = (r - 1)(c - 1)\]

One-Way ANOVA (\(F\)) & Kruskal-Wallis (\(H\)) across \(k\) groups (\(N = \sum_{g=1}^k n_g\)):

\[F = \frac{\text{SS}_{\text{between}} / (k - 1)}{\text{SS}_{\text{within}} / (N - k)}, \qquad H = \frac{12}{N(N + 1)} \sum_{g=1}^{k} \frac{R_g^2}{n_g} - 3(N + 1)\]

B.3.2 Multiple Testing, Paired Model Comparison & Bootstrap CIs

Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) False Discovery Rate (FDR) across \(m\) sorted \(p\)-values \(p_{(1)} \le \dots \le p_{(m)}\) at target FDR \(q\) (e.g., \(q = 0.05\)):

\[k^* = \max \left\{ k \in \{1, \dots, m\} : p_{(k)} \le \frac{k}{m} q \right\}\]

Reject \(H_0\) for all tests \(1, \dots, k^*\). Compared to Bonferroni (\(p_i \le \alpha / m\)), BH preserves far higher statistical power in multi-biomarker and multi-subgroup audits.

Exact McNemar’s Test for comparing two classifiers on the exact same test set (Chapter 33), using discordant counts \(b\) (Model A right, Model B wrong) and \(c\) (Model A wrong, Model B right):

\[p_{\text{exact}} = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{\min(b, c)} \binom{b + c}{k} (0.5)^{b + c}, \qquad \chi^2_{\text{cc}} = \frac{(|b - c| - 1)^2}{b + c}\]

Non-Parametric Percentile Bootstrap 95% CI (\(B \ge 2{,}000\) resamples with replacement):

\[\text{CI}_{95\%}(\hat{\theta}) = \bigl[\,\hat{\theta}^*_{(0.025)},\; \hat{\theta}^*_{(0.975)}\,\bigr]\]

B.4 Classical ML Objectives & Regularization (Chapters 22–26)

B.4.1 Regression Loss Functions, \(R^2\) & Regularized Linear Models

\[\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2, \quad \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\text{MSE}}, \quad \text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i - \hat{y}_i|, \quad R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}\]

OLS Normal Equation & Penalized Objectives (Chapter 22):

\[\hat{w}_{\text{OLS}} = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y \qquad \text{(solve } X^\top X w = X^\top y \text{ via QR/SVD)}\]

\[\mathcal{J}_{\text{Ridge}}(w) = \frac{1}{n}\|Xw - y\|_2^2 + \lambda \|w\|_2^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \hat{w}_{\text{Ridge}} = (X^\top X + n\lambda I)^{-1} X^\top y\]

\[\mathcal{J}_{\text{Lasso}}(w) = \frac{1}{2n}\|Xw - y\|_2^2 + \lambda \|w\|_1\]

\[\mathcal{J}_{\text{ElasticNet}}(w) = \frac{1}{2n}\|Xw - y\|_2^2 + \lambda \left(\rho \|w\|_1 + \frac{1 - \rho}{2}\|w\|_2^2\right)\]

B.4.2 Logistic Regression, Binary Cross-Entropy & Gradient Cancellation

With sigmoid \(\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}\), predicted probability \(\hat{p}_i = \sigma(z_i)\), and \(z_i = x_i^\top w + b\):

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \Bigl[\, y_i \ln \hat{p}_i + (1 - y_i) \ln(1 - \hat{p}_i) \,\Bigr]\]

Because \(\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))\) cancels the denominator of \(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \hat{p}_i}\), the logit gradient collapses to a remarkably clean residual (Chapters 23, 27):

\[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_i}{\partial z_i} = \hat{p}_i - y_i, \qquad \nabla_w \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = \frac{1}{n} X^\top (\hat{p} - y)\]

B.4.3 Tree Impurity, Exact Shapley Values & Probability Calibration

Decision Tree Node Impurity across class proportions \(p_1, \dots, p_C\) (Chapter 24):

\[G(t) = 1 - \sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c^2 \quad \text{(Gini)}, \qquad H(t) = -\sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c \log_2 p_c \quad \text{(Shannon Entropy)}\]

Exact Shapley Value for feature \(j\) in feature set \(F\) (Chapter 24):

\[\phi_j(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|! \, (|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} \Bigl[\, v(S \cup \{j\}) - v(S) \,\Bigr]\]

  • Local Accuracy Guarantee: \(\sum_{j \in F} \phi_j = f(x) - \mathbb{E}[f(X)]\), meaning feature attributions sum exactly to the gap between a patient’s prediction and the population base rate.

Brier Score & Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across \(B\) probability bins (Chapters 23, 34):

\[\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\hat{p}_i - y_i)^2, \qquad \text{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} \frac{|B_b|}{n} \Bigl|\operatorname{acc}(B_b) - \operatorname{conf}(B_b)\Bigr|\]

B.5 Deep Learning & Research Evaluation Equations (Chapters 27–36)

B.5.1 2-Layer MLP Backpropagation Chain Rule (Chapter 27)

For hidden pre-activation \(z^{[1]} = W^{[1]} x + b^{[1]}\), activation \(a^{[1]} = g(z^{[1]})\), output logit \(z^{[2]} = W^{[2]} a^{[1]} + b^{[2]}\), and binary probability \(\hat{p} = \sigma(z^{[2]})\) under BCE loss:

\[\delta^{[2]} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z^{[2]}} = \hat{p} - y, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W^{[2]}} = \frac{1}{n} \delta^{[2]} (a^{[1]})^\top, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b^{[2]}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta^{[2]}_i\]

\[\delta^{[1]} = \Bigl((W^{[2]})^\top \delta^{[2]}\Bigr) \odot g'(z^{[1]}), \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W^{[1]}} = \frac{1}{n} \delta^{[1]} X^\top\]

B.5.2 Variance-Preserving Weight Initialization (Chapter 29)

\[\text{Xavier / Glorot (Tanh, Sigmoid): } \operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}}, \qquad \text{He / Kaiming (ReLU): } \operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{\text{in}}}\]

B.5.3 2D Convolution Output Shape Formula (Chapter 30)

For input height \(H\), padding \(P\), kernel size \(K\), dilation \(D\) (default \(D = 1\)), and stride \(S\):

\[H_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{H + 2P - D(K - 1) - 1}{S} \right\rfloor + 1 \;\;\xrightarrow{\,D=1\,}\;\; H_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{H + 2P - K}{S} \right\rfloor + 1\]

B.5.4 Scaled Dot-Product Attention & Low-Rank Adaptation (Chapter 32)

\[\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right) V\]

where \(M_{ij} = 0\) for allowed positions and \(M_{ij} = -\infty\) for masked (padding or future causal) tokens. Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) restores unit dot-product variance (\(\operatorname{Var}(q \cdot k / \sqrt{d_k}) = 1\)), preventing softmax saturation.

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) freezes \(W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}\) and learns rank-\(r\) factors \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}\) (\(B = 0\) at step 0):

\[W_{\text{eff}} = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A, \qquad \text{Trainable params} = r(d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}) \ll d_{\text{in}} d_{\text{out}}\]

B.5.5 Algorithmic Fairness, Distribution Drift & Clinical Utility (Chapters 34–36)

Subgroup Fairness Disparity Metrics across sensitive groups \(g \in \mathcal{G}\) (Chapter 34):

\[\Delta_{\text{DP}} = \max_{g} P(\hat{Y}=1 \mid G=g) - \min_{g} P(\hat{Y}=1 \mid G=g)\]

\[\Delta_{\text{TPR}} = \max_{g} \text{TPR}_g - \min_{g} \text{TPR}_g, \qquad \Delta_{\text{EO}} = \max\bigl(\Delta_{\text{TPR}},\; \Delta_{\text{FPR}}\bigr)\]

Population Stability Index (PSI) comparing reference baseline bin shares \(p_b\) to production/deployment shares \(q_b\) (Chapters 34–35):

\[\text{PSI} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} (q_b - p_b) \ln\!\left(\frac{q_b}{p_b}\right)\]

  • Drift Triage Thresholds: \(\text{PSI} < 0.10\) (stable distribution), \(0.10 \le \text{PSI} \le 0.25\) (moderate shift — investigate covariates), \(\text{PSI} > 0.25\) (major distribution drift — re-calibrate or retrain).

Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) Net Benefit at clinical decision threshold \(p_t \in (0, 1)\) (Chapters 34, 36):

\[\text{NB}(p_t) = \frac{\text{TP}}{N} - \frac{\text{FP}}{N}\left(\frac{p_t}{1 - p_t}\right), \qquad \text{NB}_{\text{Treat-All}}(p_t) = \pi - (1 - \pi)\left(\frac{p_t}{1 - p_t}\right)\]

where \(\pi\) is outcome prevalence and \(\text{NB}_{\text{Treat-None}} = 0\). A prediction model delivers clinical value at threshold \(p_t\) only when \(\text{NB}(p_t) > \max\bigl(0,\; \text{NB}_{\text{Treat-All}}(p_t)\bigr)\).
