29  Training Dynamics, Optimizers, Regularization, BatchNorm, and Early Stopping

Chapter Mission: Transform fragile neural networks that either vanish, explode, or overfit small clinical datasets into stable, reproducible, well-regularized deep learning pipelines. Using Bangladesh maternal and child health survey data (mch_survey_sample) in PyTorch, you will diagnose vanishing and exploding activation variance, pair Kaiming (He) and Xavier (Glorot) initialization with the right nonlinearities, benchmark SGD, SGD + Momentum, and AdamW from identical initial weights, combine BatchNorm1d and Dropout safely across train() vs. eval() modes, tame spikes with gradient norm clipping, schedule learning rates with CosineAnnealingLR, and halt overfitting with deep-copied early-stopping checkpoint restoration.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Diagnose vanishing and exploding activations/gradients across deep layers and select Kaiming (He) initialization for ReLU networks vs. Xavier (Glorot) initialization for symmetric activations (tanh / sigmoid).
  2. Benchmark SGD, SGD with Momentum, and AdamW fairly from cloned initial state_dict() weights, explaining why fast adaptive optimizers require explicit regularization on small clinical cohorts.
  3. Combine nn.BatchNorm1d and nn.Dropout inside a reusable nn.Module returning raw logits, and verify the mandatory model.train() vs. model.eval() contract under torch.no_grad().
  4. Protect optimization against gradient spikes using global L2 gradient norm clipping (nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_) without altering the descent direction.
  5. Schedule smooth learning-rate decay with CosineAnnealingLR and audit how decoupled weight decay in AdamW shrinks weight-matrix L2 norms monotonically.
  6. Build an EarlyStoppingTracker that tracks patience and min_delta, deep-copies model.state_dict() at every validation minimum, and restores the optimal checkpoint before untouched test evaluation.

29.1 Stage 1 — First-Principles Intuition: Why Deep Networks Fail Without Stabilization

Writing a forward pass and calling loss.backward() is only half of deep learning. Even a modest multi-layer perceptron can silently fail in three classic ways:

  • Signal Collapse or Explosion at Depth: Multiplying activations through 10 linear layers compounds any variance mismatch exponentially. If initial weights are slightly too small (\(\sigma = 0.02\)), activations collapse to 0.000000; if slightly too large (\(\sigma = 0.35\)), activations explode past 21,000 before epoch 1 even finishes!
  • Fast Optimization Without Regularization Equals Fast Overfitting: Adaptive optimizers such as AdamW rapidly drive training loss down—which is wonderful on millions of rows, yet on a 295-row clinical training split (mch_survey_sample), an unregularized MLP memorizes noise within 40 epochs and watches validation AUC collapse below random guessing!
  • Silent Mode & Checkpointing Bugs: Forgetting model.eval() leaves Dropout randomly masking neurons and BatchNorm1d normalizing test patients by batch noise during clinical inference. Worse, saving best_sd = model.state_dict() without copy.deepcopy(...) saves live tensor views that overwrite on the very next optimizer.step().

Five first-principles pillars anchor stable neural network training:

  • Variance-Preserving Initialization: For a layer with \(n_{\text{in}}\) inputs, Kaiming (He) normal sets \(\text{Var}(W) = 2 / n_{\text{in}}\) so ReLU’s half-rectification preserves \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) signal variance across depth, whereas Xavier (Glorot) uniform balances fan-in and fan-out via \(\text{Var}(W) = 2 / (n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}})\) for symmetric activations.
  • Momentum, Adaptive Steps & Decoupled Weight Decay: Plain SGD zig-zags across narrow ravines; Momentum accumulates an exponential velocity average; AdamW pairs per-parameter adaptive step scaling with decoupled weight decay (\(\theta \leftarrow (1 - \eta \lambda)\theta - \eta \hat{m} / (\sqrt{\hat{v}} + \varepsilon)\)), shrinking weight matrices cleanly without distorting gradient variance estimates.
  • BatchNorm1d & Dropout Synergy: Placing Linear -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Dropout -> Linear standardizes hidden pre-activations (smoothing the loss landscape) while randomly zeroing hidden units during train() so no single neuron memorizes a quirk of one clinic or household.
  • Global Gradient Clipping & Cosine LR Schedules: clip_grad_norm_(params, max_norm) rescales the entire gradient vector whenever \(\|\mathbf{g}\|_2 > c\), keeping direction intact while blocking NaN explosions. Pairing this with CosineAnnealingLR starts with bold exploration and smoothly settles into a flat minimum.
  • Early Stopping with Deep-Copy Restoration: Monitoring validation loss on a dedicated validation split (60/20/20 train/val/test) stops training once patience consecutive epochs fail to improve by min_delta—and restores the deep-copied best_state_dict before touching the held-out test set.
NoteMental Model — Every Training Run Has Two Separate Goals

Think of neural training as two coupled control loops:

  1. Optimization Stability (Making Training Loss Fall Smoothly): Kaiming initialization, BatchNorm1d, AdamW, gradient clipping, and CosineAnnealingLR.
  2. Generalization Control (Keeping Validation Loss Low): Decoupled weight_decay, Dropout, train-only StandardScaler, and EarlyStoppingTracker restoring the best deep-copied checkpoint before test evaluation.

WarningTwo Silent PyTorch Traps That Never Throw an Error
  1. Shallow state_dict() Aliasing: Writing best_sd = model.state_dict() copies only dictionary keys pointing to the live parameter tensors. Every subsequent optimizer.step() overwrites best_sd in place! Always wrap it in copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict()).
  2. Target Leakage in mch_survey_sample: Never feed maternal_hb_g_dl when predicting maternal_anemia (WHO defines anemia as Hb < 11.0 g/dL), and never fit StandardScaler before splitting 60/20/20.

29.3.1 Setup: Clean mch_survey_sample, Stratified 60/20/20 Split & Train-Only Scaling

import copy
import math
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
import sharukh_tutor as tutor

torch.set_num_threads(1)
mch_raw = tutor.load_data("mch_survey_sample", as_pandas=True)
mch = mch_raw.copy()
mch["residence"] = (
    mch["residence"].astype(str).str.strip().str.title()
)
mch["anc_visits"] = mch["anc_visits"].replace(999, np.nan)
mch["is_urban"] = (mch["residence"] == "Urban").astype(int)
wealth_map = {
    "Poorest": 1, "Poorer": 2, "Middle": 3,
    "Richer": 4, "Richest": 5,
}
mch["wealth_num"] = mch["wealth_quintile"].map(wealth_map)
feat_cols = [
    "maternal_age_years", "anc_visits", "wealth_num", "is_urban"
]
clean = mch[["maternal_anemia"] + feat_cols].dropna()
X_all = clean[feat_cols].to_numpy(dtype=np.float32)
y_all = clean["maternal_anemia"].astype(int).to_numpy()

X_tr_raw, X_tmp, y_tr, y_tmp = train_test_split(
    X_all, y_all, test_size=0.40, stratify=y_all, random_state=42
)
X_va_raw, X_te_raw, y_va, y_te = train_test_split(
    X_tmp, y_tmp, test_size=0.50, stratify=y_tmp, random_state=42

scaler = StandardScaler()
X_tr = scaler.fit_transform(X_tr_raw)
X_va = scaler.transform(X_va_raw)
X_te = scaler.transform(X_te_raw)

Xtr_t = torch.tensor(X_tr, dtype=torch.float32)

Xva_t = torch.tensor(X_va, dtype=torch.float32)
yva_t = torch.tensor(y_va, dtype=torch.float32).unsqueeze(1)
Xte_t = torch.tensor(X_te, dtype=torch.float32)
print(
    f"Split sizes -> train={len(y_tr)}, val={len(y_va)}, "
    f"test={len(y_te)} | Anemia={y_all.mean():.4f}"
)

Expected Output:

Split sizes -> train=295, val=98, test=99 | Anemia=0.3537

Notice two crucial hygiene habits: StandardScaler fits strictly on X_tr_raw (295 mothers) before transforming X_va_raw (98) and X_te_raw (99), and labels are reshaped with .unsqueeze(1) to (N, 1) so they match the (N, 1) logits returned by nn.Linear(..., 1) without silent broadcasting bugs inside BCEWithLogitsLoss. Calling torch.set_num_threads(1) avoids OpenMP thread-pool overhead on small tabular batches, keeping CPU runs fast and deterministic.

29.3.2 Vanishing/Exploding Variance & He (Kaiming) vs. Xavier Initialization

Watch what happens to the standard deviation of hidden activations as a (64, 128) standard-normal batch passes through 10 consecutive Linear(128, 128) -> ReLU layers under four initialization scales:

# Forward activation variance across 10 ReLU layers (width=128)
for label, mode in [
    ("Too small (std=0.02)", 0.02),
    ("Kaiming ReLU", "kaiming"),
    ("Xavier Uniform", "xavier"),
    ("Too large (std=0.35)", 0.35),
]:
    torch.manual_seed(42)
    h = torch.randn(64, 128)
    for _ in range(10):
        layer = nn.Linear(128, 128, bias=False)
        if mode == "kaiming":
            nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, nonlinearity="relu")
        elif mode == "xavier":
            nn.init.xavier_uniform_(layer.weight)
        else:
            nn.init.normal_(layer.weight, mean=0.0, std=mode)
        h = torch.relu(layer(h))
    print(f"{label:21s} | 10-layer act std = {h.std().item():.6f}")

Expected Output:

Too small (std=0.02)  | 10-layer act std = 0.000000
Kaiming ReLU          | 10-layer act std = 0.721697
Xavier Uniform        | 10-layer act std = 0.021917
Too large (std=0.35)  | 10-layer act std = 21376.416016

Stare at those four lines—this is why initialization theory exists:

  • Too small (std = 0.02): Signal variance shrinks by a factor of \((128 \times 0.02^2 / 2)^{10} \approx 10^{-16}\), collapsing to 0.000000 (vanishing activations and gradients).
  • Xavier Uniform on ReLU (0.021917): Xavier sets \(\text{Var}(W) = 1 / n\) assuming linear/symmetric activations (tanh), so ReLU zeroing half the units halves the variance at every layer: \((1/2)^{10/2} \approx 0.031\).
  • Kaiming ReLU (0.721697): Multiplying variance by 2 (\(\text{Var}(W) = 2 / n_{\text{in}}\)) exactly cancels ReLU’s half-rectification, keeping activations healthy and \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) even 10 layers deep!
  • Too large (std = 0.35): Activations blow up past 21,376, guaranteeing overflowing NaN gradients on the first backward pass.

29.3.3 Controlled Optimizer Benchmark: SGD vs. Momentum vs. AdamW

To compare optimizers fairly, we snapshot identical initial weights (base_sd = copy.deepcopy(base_mlp.state_dict())) and train an unregularized 2-layer MLP for 40 epochs on mch_survey_sample:

torch.manual_seed(42)
base_mlp = nn.Sequential(
    nn.Linear(4, 32), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(32, 1)
)
base_sd = copy.deepcopy(base_mlp.state_dict())
crit = nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss()

configs = [
    ("SGD", lambda p: torch.optim.SGD(p, lr=0.05)),
    ("SGD+Momentum", lambda p: torch.optim.SGD(p, lr=0.05, momentum=0.9)),
    ("AdamW", lambda p: torch.optim.AdamW(p, lr=0.01, weight_decay=1e-2)),
]
for name, build_opt in configs:
    m = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(4, 32), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(32, 1))
    m.load_state_dict(copy.deepcopy(base_sd))
    opt = build_opt(m.parameters())
    for _ in range(40):
        m.train()
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss = crit(m(Xtr_t), ytr_t)
        loss.backward()
        opt.step()
    m.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        tr_l = float(crit(m(Xtr_t), ytr_t).item())
        va_l = float(crit(m(Xva_t), yva_t).item())
        va_p = torch.sigmoid(m(Xva_t)).squeeze(1).numpy()
        va_auc = roc_auc_score(y_va, va_p)
    print(
        f"{name:13s} | train={tr_l:.4f} | "
        f"val={va_l:.4f} | val_auc={va_auc:.4f}"
    )

Expected Output:

SGD           | train=0.6278 | val=0.6457 | val_auc=0.5751
SGD+Momentum  | train=0.6085 | val=0.6619 | val_auc=0.5592
AdamW         | train=0.5967 | val=0.7007 | val_auc=0.4844

Look closely at the two lessons hidden in this table:

  • Optimization speed: From the exact same starting weights, SGD+Momentum (0.6085) and AdamW (0.5967) push training loss down far faster than vanilla SGD (0.6278).
  • The small-data overfitting cliff: Because this bare MLP has no BatchNorm, no Dropout, and no Early Stopping, AdamW’s superior optimization speed actually drives it straight past the validation optimum (val_loss = 0.7007, val_auc = 0.4844)! Speed without regularization and early stopping hurts generalization.

29.3.4 BatchNorm1d & The Dropout train() vs. eval() Trap

Now wrap hidden activations in BatchNorm1d + Dropout inside ClinicalRegNet, and directly verify how model.train() differs from model.eval() on the exact same batch:

class ClinicalRegNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_dim, hidden_dim=32, dropout_p=0.3):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(in_dim, hidden_dim),
            nn.BatchNorm1d(hidden_dim),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Dropout(dropout_p),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 1),
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        return self.net(x)

torch.manual_seed(42)
reg_net = ClinicalRegNet(in_dim=4, hidden_dim=32, dropout_p=0.3)
with torch.no_grad():
    reg_net.train()
    t1, t2 = reg_net(Xva_t[:8]), reg_net(Xva_t[:8])
    reg_net.eval()
    e1, e2 = reg_net(Xva_t[:8]), reg_net(Xva_t[:8])
print(f"train() max |t1 - t2| = {(t1 - t2).abs().max().item():.5f}")
print(f"eval()  max |e1 - e2| = {(e1 - e2).abs().max().item():.5f}")

Expected Output:

train() max |t1 - t2| = 0.51361
eval()  max |e1 - e2| = 0.00000

In model.train() mode, Dropout(0.3) randomly zeroes 30% of hidden activations and rescales survivors by \(1 / (1 - 0.3)\) (inverted dropout), producing a 0.51361 discrepancy between two back-to-back calls on identical patients! Switching to model.eval() disables dropout and switches BatchNorm1d to its stored running mean and variance—yielding exact 0.00000 determinism.

29.3.5 Gradient Norm Clipping & Cosine Annealing LR Schedule

When an outlier batch or deep recurrence produces a massive gradient norm \(\|\mathbf{g}\|_2\), global gradient norm clipping rescales all parameter gradients uniformly by \(c / \|\mathbf{g}\|_2\) whenever \(\|\mathbf{g}\|_2 > c\), preserving descent direction while capping step size. Meanwhile, CosineAnnealingLR smoothly anneals the learning rate from \(\eta_0\) toward \(\eta_{\min} = 0\) via \(\eta_t = \frac{1}{2}\eta_0(1 + \cos(\pi t / T_{\max}))\):

# 1. Gradient clipping on an artificially amplified loss
torch.manual_seed(42)
clip_net = ClinicalRegNet(in_dim=4, hidden_dim=32, dropout_p=0.3)
clip_opt = torch.optim.AdamW(clip_net.parameters(), lr=0.01)
clip_opt.zero_grad()
big_loss = crit(clip_net(Xtr_t), ytr_t) * 25.0
big_loss.backward()
norm_before = float(
    nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(clip_net.parameters(), max_norm=1.0)
)
norm_after = math.sqrt(sum(
    float(p.grad.detach().pow(2).sum().item())
    for p in clip_net.parameters() if p.grad is not None
))
clip_opt.step()
print(
    f"Grad L2 norm -> before={norm_before:.4f} | "
    f"after={norm_after:.4f}"
)

# 2. Smooth CosineAnnealingLR decay across 20 epochs
dummy = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(1))
lr_opt = torch.optim.SGD([dummy], lr=0.02)
sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(lr_opt, T_max=20)
lrs = []
for _ in range(20):
    lrs.append(round(float(lr_opt.param_groups[0]["lr"]), 6))
    lr_opt.step()
    sched.step()
print(
    f"Cosine LR -> ep0={lrs[0]:.6f}, "
    f"ep10={lrs[10]:.6f}, ep19={lrs[-1]:.6f}"
)

Expected Output:

Grad L2 norm -> before=11.4680 | after=1.0000
Cosine LR -> ep0=0.020000, ep10=0.010000, ep19=0.000123

Notice two exact mathematical fingerprints:

  • An 11.4680 gradient spike was rescaled cleanly to 1.0000 right before clip_opt.step().
  • At midpoint ep10 = T_max / 2, \(\cos(\pi / 2) = 0\), so the cosine learning rate sits at exactly half of base_lr (0.020000 -> 0.010000), then tapers gently to 0.000123 on the final epoch!

29.3.6 Early Stopping & Restoring the Best Validation Checkpoint

Now combine BatchNorm1d + Dropout + AdamW + Gradient Clipping + CosineAnnealingLR + Early Stopping on mch_survey_sample and compare against our earlier overfit AdamW run (val_auc = 0.4844):

torch.manual_seed(42)
es_net = ClinicalRegNet(in_dim=4, hidden_dim=32, dropout_p=0.3)
es_opt = torch.optim.AdamW(es_net.parameters(), lr=0.02, weight_decay=1e-2)
es_sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(es_opt, T_max=80)

best_loss, best_ep, wait, best_sd = float("inf"), 0, 0, None
for ep in range(1, 81):
    es_net.train()
    es_opt.zero_grad()
    crit(es_net(Xtr_t), ytr_t).backward()
    nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(es_net.parameters(), max_norm=1.0)
    es_opt.step()
    es_sched.step()

    es_net.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        vloss = float(crit(es_net(Xva_t), yva_t).item())
    if vloss < best_loss:
        best_loss, best_ep, wait = vloss, ep, 0
        best_sd = copy.deepcopy(es_net.state_dict())
    else:
        wait += 1
        if wait >= 8:
            break

es_net.load_state_dict(best_sd)
es_net.eval()
with torch.no_grad():
    te_probs = torch.sigmoid(es_net(Xte_t)).squeeze(1).numpy()
te_auc = roc_auc_score(y_te, te_probs)
print(
    f"Stopped at ep={ep} (best_ep={best_ep}) | "
    f"best_val={best_loss:.5f} | test_auc={te_auc:.4f}"
)

Expected Output:

Stopped at ep=12 (best_ep=4) | best_val=0.64933 | test_auc=0.6853

What a dramatic turnaround! Instead of overfitting for 40 epochs (val_loss = 0.7007, AUC = 0.4844), early stopping halted training at epoch 12, rewound the model to its deep-copied epoch-4 checkpoint (best_val = 0.64933), and achieved test_auc = 0.6853 on the untouched 99-mother test cohort!

29.3.7 Break It On Purpose! (Training Dynamics Error Anatomy)

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #1 — ValueError (BatchNorm1d Crash on Batch Size 1 in train() Mode) 
bn = nn.BatchNorm1d(8)
bn.train()
bn(torch.randn(1, 8))

Python’s Traceback:

ValueError: Expected more than 1 value per channel when training,
got input size torch.Size([1, 8])

Diagnosis: During train(), BatchNorm1d estimates unbiased per-channel variance using denominator \(B - 1\). When a mini-batch has size \(B = 1\) (for example, the leftover final batch of a DataLoader when len(dataset) % batch_size == 1, or single-patient inference without model.eval()), \(B - 1 = 0\) makes batch variance undefined!
Fix: Pass drop_last=True to your training DataLoader (so no training batch has size 1), and always call model.eval() before running single-patient inference (so BatchNorm1d uses its stored running statistics instead of batch statistics).

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #2 — ValueError (Negative Learning Rate in AdamW) 
torch.optim.AdamW([nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(2))], lr=-0.01)

Python’s Traceback:

ValueError: Invalid learning rate: -0.01

Diagnosis: PyTorch optimizers validate hyperparameters eagerly at construction time and reject negative learning rates (which would perform gradient ascent instead of descent) or negative weight_decay. This error commonly surfaces when a buggy custom LR schedule or log-scale hyperparameter grid passes a negative value.
Fix: Keep learning rates strictly non-negative (lr > 0), sample learning rates on a log-uniform scale (10 ** np.random.uniform(-4, -1)), and rely on built-in schedulers like CosineAnnealingLR.

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #3 — ValueError (Invalid Dropout Probability \(> 1.0\)) 
nn.Dropout(p=1.5)

Python’s Traceback:

ValueError: dropout probability has to be between 0 and 1,
but got 1.5

Diagnosis: nn.Dropout(p) specifies the fraction of activations zeroed during training, which must satisfy \(0 \le p \le 1\). Passing a percentage like 30 instead of 0.3, or mis-reading a hyperparameter config file, triggers an immediate ValueError.
Fix: Express dropout as a probability in [0.0, 1.0]—typically 0.1–0.3 for tabular clinical MLPs and 0.3–0.5 for wider hidden layers—and remember p is the drop probability, not the keep probability.

29.8 Stage 8 — Complete Annotated Solutions & Debugging Post-Mortem

29.8.1 A. Concept & Execution Trace Test Key

  1. Kaiming: \(\sqrt{2 / 200} = \sqrt{0.01} = \mathbf{0.1000}\). Xavier: \(\sqrt{2 / (200 + 200)} = \sqrt{0.005} \approx \mathbf{0.0707}\). Kaiming is \(\sqrt{2}\times\) larger because ReLU zeroes the negative half of a zero-mean distribution, halving signal variance at every layer.
  2. Activation variance shrinks by \((0.5)^{10} = 1 / 1024 \approx \mathbf{0.000977}\)—losing over 99.9% of signal variance!
  3. Initialization variance confounder: Each optimizer started at a different random point on the non-convex loss surface, so luck of initialization is confounded with optimizer performance. Cloning copy.deepcopy(base.state_dict()) isolates the optimizer effect.
  4. AdamW decouples weight decay from the gradient moment estimates, shrinking weights directly via \(\theta_t \leftarrow (1 - \eta \lambda)\theta_t\) before applying the adaptive Adam step—guaranteeing uniform, predictable L2 shrinkage across all parameters.
  5. In train() mode, BatchNorm1d computes batch variance with denominator \(B - 1 = 0\) when \(B = 1\), raising ValueError. In eval() mode, it normalizes using its saved running_mean and running_var, which works for any batch size including \(B = 1\).
  6. Surviving activations are scaled by \(1 / (1 - p) = 1 / 0.5 = \mathbf{2.0}\) during train() so their expected magnitude matches the unmasked eval() forward pass automatically.
  7. Original norm \(\|\mathbf{g}\|_2 = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5.0 > 1.0\). Scaling by \(1.0 / 5.0\) gives \(\tilde{\mathbf{g}} = \mathbf{[0.6, 0.8]}\) with norm 1.0. Because every component shares the same positive scalar factor, the direction is completely unchanged.
  8. It holds the final (overfit) epoch’s weights! Because model.state_dict() returns live tensor references, every optimizer.step() silently overwrote best_state_dict in place.
  9. Using \(\eta_t = \frac{1}{2}\eta_0(1 + \cos(\pi t / T_{\max}))\): at \(t = 0\), \(\eta_0 = \mathbf{0.04}\); at \(t = 10\), \(\eta_{10} = 0.02(1 + 0) = \mathbf{0.02}\); at \(t = 20\), \(\eta_{20} = 0.02(1 - 1) = \mathbf{0.00}\).
  10. Epoch 1 (0.80): best_loss = 0.80, counter = 0. Epoch 2 (0.79): 0.79 < 0.80 - 0.02 = 0.78 is False, so counter = 1. Epoch 3 (0.785): still not < 0.78, so counter = 2 >= patience, setting should_stop = True on Epoch 3!

import copy
import math
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

# To-Do 29.1
def init_linear_weights(layer, method="kaiming"):
    """Initialize a Linear layer with Kaiming or Xavier weights."""
    if method == "kaiming":
        nn.init.kaiming_normal_(layer.weight, nonlinearity="relu")
    elif method == "xavier":
        nn.init.xavier_uniform_(layer.weight)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown initialization method: {method!r}")
    if layer.bias is not None:
        nn.init.zeros_(layer.bias)
    return layer

# To-Do 29.2
class RegularizedClinicalMLP(nn.Module):
    """Regularized MLP with BatchNorm1d, ReLU, and Dropout."""

    def __init__(self, in_dim, hidden_dim=32, dropout_p=0.3):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(in_dim, hidden_dim),
            nn.BatchNorm1d(hidden_dim),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Dropout(dropout_p),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 1),
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        return self.net(x)

# To-Do 29.3
def verify_dropout_train_vs_eval(model, x_batch):
    """Check stochastic train() vs deterministic eval() forward passes."""
    with torch.no_grad():
        model.train()
        t1 = model(x_batch)
        t2 = model(x_batch)
        model.eval()
        e1 = model(x_batch)
        e2 = model(x_batch)
    return {
        "train_stochastic": bool(not torch.allclose(t1, t2)),
        "eval_deterministic": bool(torch.allclose(e1, e2)),
    }

# To-Do 29.4
def step_with_grad_clipping(model, loss, optimizer, max_norm=1.0):
    """Zero grads, backprop, clip global L2 norm, and step optimizer."""
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    norm_before = float(
        nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), max_norm)
    )
    sq_sum = sum(
        float(p.grad.detach().pow(2).sum().item())
        for p in model.parameters()
        if p.grad is not None
    )
    norm_after = math.sqrt(sq_sum)
    optimizer.step()
    return {
        "grad_norm_before": round(norm_before, 4),
        "grad_norm_after": round(norm_after, 4),
    }

# To-Do 29.5
class EarlyStoppingTracker:
    """Track validation loss, deep-copy best weights, and signal stop."""

    def __init__(self, patience=5, min_delta=0.0):
        self.patience = patience
        self.min_delta = min_delta
        self.best_loss = float("inf")
        self.counter = 0
        self.should_stop = False
        self.best_state_dict = None

    def step(self, val_loss, model):
        if val_loss < self.best_loss - self.min_delta:
            self.best_loss = float(val_loss)
            self.counter = 0
            self.best_state_dict = copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())
        else:
            self.counter += 1
            if self.counter >= self.patience:
                self.should_stop = True
        return self.should_stop

# To-Do 29.6
def cosine_lr_schedule_trace(base_lr=0.01, t_max=20):
    """Trace smooth CosineAnnealingLR decay across t_max steps."""
    param = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(1))
    opt = torch.optim.SGD([param], lr=base_lr)
    sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(opt, T_max=t_max)
    lrs = []
    for _ in range(t_max):
        lrs.append(round(float(opt.param_groups[0]["lr"]), 6))
        opt.step()
        sched.step()
    return lrs

        model = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(in_dim, 32), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(32, 1)
        )
        model.load_state_dict(copy.deepcopy(base_sd))
        opt = torch.optim.AdamW(
            model.parameters(), lr=0.01, weight_decay=wd
        )
        for _ in range(epochs):
            model.train()
            opt.zero_grad()
            crit(model(Xtr), ytr).backward()
            opt.step()
        model.eval()
        with torch.no_grad():
            vl = float(crit(model(Xva), yva).item())
            vp = torch.sigmoid(model(Xva)).squeeze(1).numpy()
            auc = float(roc_auc_score(yva_np, vp))
            l2 = math.sqrt(sum(
                float(p.detach().pow(2).sum().item())
                for p in model.parameters()
                if p.ndim >= 2
            ))
        rows.append({
            "weight_decay": round(float(wd), 4),
            "val_loss": round(vl, 4),
            "val_auc": round(auc, 4),
            "l2_weight_norm": round(l2, 4),
        })
    cols = ["weight_decay", "val_loss", "val_auc", "l2_weight_norm"]
    table = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=cols)
    best_wd = float(table.loc[table["val_loss"].idxmin(), "weight_decay"])
    return {"best_wd": best_wd, "table": table}

# Exam Task 29.3 (35 pts)
def train_with_early_stopping_mch(
    mch_df, patience=8, max_epochs=80, seed=42
):
    """Train leak-free regularized MCH MLP with early stopping."""
    work = mch_df.copy()
    work["residence"] = (
        work["residence"].astype(str).str.strip().str.title()
    )
    work["anc_visits"] = work["anc_visits"].replace(999, np.nan)
    work["is_urban"] = (work["residence"] == "Urban").astype(int)
    w_map = {
        "Poorest": 1, "Poorer": 2, "Middle": 3,
        "Richer": 4, "Richest": 5,
    }
    work["wealth_num"] = work["wealth_quintile"].map(w_map)
    feat_cols = [
        "maternal_age_years", "anc_visits", "wealth_num", "is_urban"
    ]
    clean = work[["maternal_anemia"] + feat_cols].dropna()
    X = clean[feat_cols].to_numpy(dtype=np.float32)
    y = clean["maternal_anemia"].astype(int).to_numpy()

    Xtr, Xtmp, ytr, ytmp = train_test_split(
        X, y, test_size=0.40, stratify=y, random_state=seed
    )
    Xva, Xte, yva, yte = train_test_split(
        Xtmp, ytmp, test_size=0.50, stratify=ytmp, random_state=seed
    )
    scaler = StandardScaler()
    Xtr = scaler.fit_transform(Xtr)
    Xva = scaler.transform(Xva)
    Xte = scaler.transform(Xte)

    Xtr_t = torch.tensor(Xtr, dtype=torch.float32)
    ytr_t = torch.tensor(ytr, dtype=torch.float32).unsqueeze(1)
    Xva_t = torch.tensor(Xva, dtype=torch.float32)
    yva_t = torch.tensor(yva, dtype=torch.float32).unsqueeze(1)
    Xte_t = torch.tensor(Xte, dtype=torch.float32)

    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    model = nn.Sequential(
        nn.Linear(4, 32),
        nn.BatchNorm1d(32),
        nn.ReLU(),
        nn.Dropout(0.3),
        nn.Linear(32, 1),
    )
    opt = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=0.02, weight_decay=1e-2)
    sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(
        opt, T_max=max_epochs
    )
    crit = nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss()

    best_loss, counter, stopped_early = float("inf"), 0, False
    best_sd = None
    epochs_run = 0
    for ep in range(1, max_epochs + 1):
        epochs_run = ep
        model.train()
        opt.zero_grad()
        crit(model(Xtr_t), ytr_t).backward()
        opt.step()
        sched.step()

        model.eval()
        with torch.no_grad():
            vloss = float(crit(model(Xva_t), yva_t).item())
        if vloss < best_loss:
            best_loss = vloss
            counter = 0
            best_sd = copy.deepcopy(model.state_dict())
        else:
            counter += 1
            if counter >= patience:
                stopped_early = True
                break

    if best_sd is not None:
        model.load_state_dict(best_sd)
    model.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        probs = torch.sigmoid(model(Xte_t)).squeeze(1).numpy()
    return {
        "epochs_run": int(epochs_run),
        "best_val_loss": round(float(best_loss), 5),
        "test_auc": round(float(roc_auc_score(yte, probs)), 4),
        "stopped_early": bool(stopped_early),
    }

29.8.4 D. Debugging Post-Mortem: Top 3 Chapter 29 Failure Modes

  1. Forgetting model.eval() and torch.no_grad() Before Validation or Inference: Leaving Dropout and BatchNorm1d in train() mode injects random noise into validation loss (breaking early stopping!) and causes single-patient inference to crash with ValueError: Expected more than 1 value per channel.
  2. Shallow model.state_dict() Checkpointing Without copy.deepcopy: Storing a raw state_dict() holds live references to parameter tensors that mutate on every subsequent optimizer.step(). Always deep-copy on every new validation minimum and explicitly call model.load_state_dict(best_state_dict) before touching X_test.
  3. Double-Squashing Logits with nn.Sigmoid() + BCEWithLogitsLoss(): Appending nn.Sigmoid() to a binary classifier that already trains with nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss() restricts logits to [0, 1], flattens gradients, and ruins probability calibration. Return raw logits from forward() and apply torch.sigmoid(...) only at evaluation time.
