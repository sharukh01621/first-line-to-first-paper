25.1 Stage 1 — First-Principles Intuition: Learning Structure Without Labels

In Chapters 21–24, every model learned a mapping \(\hat{y} = f(X)\) guided by ground-truth labels—whether a patient was anemic, needed urgent referral, or had elevated blood pressure. Yet in real clinical, genomic, and public health research, labels are often missing, incomplete, or not yet defined:

  • Patient Phenotyping: Do 120 patients arriving at Upazila Health Complexes naturally fall into distinct clinical profiles (e.g., pediatric febrile cases vs. older hypertensive adults) before anyone assigns a diagnosis code?
  • Health-System Targeting: Which Bangladesh districts share similar burdens of fever, blood pressure, anemia, and non-routine triage load so health ministries can group interventions?
  • Biomarker Compression: When seven physiological measurements (age_years, weight_kg, height_cm, temp_c, systolic_bp, hemoglobin_g_dl, fever_days) are strongly correlated, how many independent biological axes actually drive 85%–90% of the variation?

That is the domain of unsupervised learning: discovering latent geometry directly from \(X\) alone. Four first principles govern every method in this chapter:

  • Distance Depends Completely on Feature Scale: Euclidean distance sums squared differences across all features, \(\|x - z\|_2^2 = \sum_j (x_j - z_j)^2\). In clinic_triage, age_years has variance 451.44 while temp_c has variance 0.60—a 752x variance gap! Without StandardScaler, clustering and PCA effectively ignore body temperature and hemoglobin.
  • No Universal “Accuracy” Metric: Without ground-truth \(y\), we evaluate cluster structure using geometric cohesion and separation—inertia (within-cluster sum of squared errors) and Silhouette score \(s \in [-1, +1]\)—always backed by clinical interpretability.
  • Different Algorithms Assume Different Shapes: K-Means and Ward assume compact, roughly spherical clusters of comparable spread and force every point into a cluster. DBSCAN traces arbitrary dense regions and explicitly isolates sparse anomalies as label -1.
  • PCA Maximizes Variance & Minimizes Reconstruction Error: Projecting standardized data onto the top \(k\) orthogonal principal components preserves the maximum possible linear variance and yields the lowest possible mean squared reconstruction error—and remarkably, on standardized data, \(\text{MSE} = 1 - \text{cumulative explained variance}\)!
NoteMental Model — Two Questions Before Picking an Unsupervised Method

Never run K-Means blindly on every unlabeled table. Ask two structural questions first:

  1. Are you grouping rows (patients/districts) or compressing columns (correlated features)? Row grouping calls for Clustering; column compression and noise reduction call for PCA.
  2. If clustering, do you expect compact spherical groups, a nested hierarchy, or dense blobs mixed with outliers? Spherical \(\rightarrow\) K-Means; nested tree \(\rightarrow\) Ward Hierarchical; irregular shapes + anomalies \(\rightarrow\) DBSCAN.

WarningTwo Classic Unsupervised Pitfalls That Invalidate Papers
  1. Counting DBSCAN Noise (-1) as a Cluster: DBSCAN assigns sparse outliers to label -1. Computing len(set(labels)) falsely counts noise as an extra cluster—always subtract {-1} before reporting n_clusters!
  2. Clustering Directly on t-SNE or UMAP Coordinates: t-SNE and UMAP warp global distances and cluster diameters to preserve local neighborhoods. Never feed t-SNE coordinates into K-Means or claim two distant t-SNE islands are quantitatively “farther apart” than two close ones.

25.3.3 K-Means From Scratch in Pure NumPy

K-Means minimizes within-cluster inertia (sum of squared Euclidean distances to assigned centroids \(\mu_c\)):

\[J = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left\| x_i - \mu_{c(i)} \right\|_2^2\]

It alternates two steps until centroids stop moving: (1) assign each point \(x_i\) to its nearest centroid, and (2) replace each centroid \(\mu_c\) with the mean of points assigned to cluster \(c\).

def kmeans_scratch_demo(X, k, max_iter=50, seed=42):
    X_arr = np.asarray(X, dtype=float)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    idx = rng.choice(len(X_arr), size=k, replace=False)
    centroids = X_arr[idx].copy()
    for _ in range(max_iter):
        dists = ((X_arr[:, None, :] - centroids[None, :, :]) ** 2)
        labels = np.argmin(dists.sum(axis=2), axis=1).astype(int)
        new_c = centroids.copy()
        for cid in range(k):
            mask = labels == cid
            if np.any(mask):
                new_c[cid] = X_arr[mask].mean(axis=0)
        if np.allclose(new_c, centroids):
            centroids = new_c
            break
        centroids = new_c
    dists = ((X_arr[:, None, :] - centroids[None, :, :]) ** 2)
    labels = np.argmin(dists.sum(axis=2), axis=1).astype(int)
    inertia = float(np.sum((X_arr - centroids[labels]) ** 2))
    return labels, centroids, inertia

sc_labels, sc_centers, sc_inertia = kmeans_scratch_demo(
    X_scaled, k=3, max_iter=50, seed=42
)
print(f"Scratch K-Means inertia: {sc_inertia:.4f}")
print(f"Sklearn K-Means inertia: {km_scl.inertia_:.4f}")
print("Scratch cluster sizes:",
      {int(c): int(n) for c, n in zip(*np.unique(
          sc_labels, return_counts=True))})

Expected Output:

Scratch K-Means inertia: 549.5317
Sklearn K-Means inertia: 514.0598
Scratch cluster sizes: {0: 29, 1: 40, 2: 51}

Pause and appreciate two key lessons in this output:

  • Vectorized Broadcasting ((n, 1, d) - (1, k, d)): One line computes every point-to-centroid squared distance in pure NumPy without a slow Python loop over patients!
  • Why Single-Start Inertia (549.5317) > Sklearn Inertia (514.0598): K-Means is non-convex—a single random initialization can converge to a suboptimal local minimum. Scikit-Learn uses k-means++ smart seeding across n_init=10 independent restarts and keeps the lowest-inertia solution (514.0598).

25.3.4 Choosing \(k\): Elbow Method & Silhouette Score

For each sample \(i\), the Silhouette coefficient compares mean intra-cluster distance \(a(i)\) against mean nearest-other-cluster distance \(b(i)\):

\[s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))} \in [-1, +1]\]

Values near +1 signal tight, well-separated clusters; values near 0 indicate overlapping borders; negative values indicate misassigned points.

sweep_rows = []
for k in range(2, 7):
    km = KMeans(n_clusters=k, n_init=10, random_state=42)
    lbl = km.fit_predict(X_scaled)
    sweep_rows.append({
        "k": k,
        "inertia": round(float(km.inertia_), 4),
        "silhouette": round(float(silhouette_score(X_scaled, lbl)), 4),
    })
k_table = pd.DataFrame(sweep_rows)
print(k_table.to_string(index=False))
best_row = k_table.loc[k_table["silhouette"].idxmax()]
print(f"Peak silhouette at k={int(best_row['k'])} "
      f"({best_row['silhouette']:.4f})")

Expected Output:

Notice the classic pattern: inertia drops monotonically (663.08 \(\rightarrow\) 331.97) because adding more centroids always shrinks within-cluster distance, eventually reaching 0 when \(k = n\). 25.3.5 Hierarchical Agglomerative Clustering (Ward Linkage)

Agglomerative clustering starts with every patient as a singleton cluster and iteratively merges the two clusters that minimize Ward’s criterion—the exact increase in total within-cluster squared variance:

ward_labels = ward.fit_predict(X_scaled)
ward_sil = silhouette_score(X_scaled, ward_labels)
ward_sizes = {
    int(c): int(n)
    for c, n in zip(*np.unique(ward_labels, return_counts=True))
}
print(f"Ward (k=3) silhouette: {ward_sil:.4f}")

Expected Output:

Ward (k=3) silhouette: 0.2847
Ward (k=3) cluster sizes: {0: 89, 1: 24, 2: 7}

25.3.6 DBSCAN: Density Clustering & Anomaly Flagging

DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) defines dense neighborhoods using two intuitive hyperparameters: radius eps and minimum neighbor count min_samples. Points in sparse regions are never forced into a cluster—they receive label -1 (noise / anomaly):

db = DBSCAN(eps=1.8, min_samples=5)
db_labels = db.fit_predict(X_scaled)
n_clusters = int(len(set(db_labels.tolist()) - {-1}))
n_noise = int(np.sum(db_labels == -1))
print(f"DBSCAN (eps=1.8, min_samples=5): "
      f"{n_clusters} clusters, {n_noise} noise outliers")
outlier_preview = clinic.loc[
    db_labels == -1, ["patient_id", "age_years", "temp_c", "systolic_bp"]
].head(3)
print("Sample flagged clinical outliers:\n",
      outlier_preview.to_string(index=False))

25.3.7 PCA From Scratch via NumPy SVD vs. Scikit-Learn

Given a mean-centered matrix \(X_c = X - \bar{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}\), its thin Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) factorizes \(X_c = U \Sigma V^\top\). The rows of \(V^\top\) are the orthogonal principal component axes (loadings), the projected coordinates are \(Z_k = X_c V_k\), and the explained variance ratio of component \(j\) is simply:

\[\text{EVR}_j = \frac{\sigma_j^2}{\sum_{m=1}^{r} \sigma_m^2}\]

# 1. PCA from scratch via centered SVD
X_c = X_scaled - X_scaled.mean(axis=0)
U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(X_c, full_matrices=False)
Z_svd = X_c @ Vt[:2].T
evr_svd = ((S ** 2) / np.sum(S ** 2))[:2]

# 2. Compare directly against sklearn.decomposition.PCA
pca_full = PCA().fit(X_scaled)
cum_var = np.cumsum(pca_full.explained_variance_ratio_)
print("SVD EVR (top 2):    ", np.round(evr_svd, 4))
print("Sklearn EVR (top 2):",
      np.round(pca_full.explained_variance_ratio_[:2], 4))
print("Cumulative variance:", np.round(cum_var, 4))
k_85 = int(np.argmax(cum_var >= 0.85) + 1)
print(f"PCs required for >=85% variance: {k_85} "
      f"(retains {cum_var[k_85 - 1]:.4f})")

# 3. Interpret clinical loadings on PC1 & PC2
loadings = pd.DataFrame(
    pca_full.components_[:2].T,
    index=feat_cols,
    columns=["PC1", "PC2"],
).round(4)
print("Top-2 PC Clinical Loadings:\n", loadings)

25.3.8 Nonlinear Projections (t-SNE / UMAP) Caveats

When visualizing high-dimensional datasets (e.g., single-cell RNA-seq or multi-biomarker panels), researchers frequently reach for t-SNE or UMAP. Unlike PCA, these methods optimize a nonlinear objective preserving local neighbor probabilities—which comes with strict scientific caveats:

# t-SNE preserves local neighborhoods, NOT global distances or variance
for perp in [10, 30]:
    emb = TSNE(
        n_components=2, perplexity=perp, init="pca",
        learning_rate="auto", random_state=42,
    ).fit_transform(X_scaled)
    spread = [round(float(v), 2) for v in emb.std(axis=0)]
    print(f"t-SNE (perplexity={perp:2d}) embedding shape={emb.shape}, "
          f"axis SDs={spread}")

Expected Output:

t-SNE (perplexity=10) embedding shape=(120, 2), axis SDs=[14.46, 16.49]
t-SNE (perplexity=30) embedding shape=(120, 2), axis SDs=[3.22, 4.36]

Changing a single hyperparameter (perplexity=10 vs. 30) shrinks the embedding coordinate spread by nearly 4x ([14.46, 16.49] vs. [3.22, 4.36])! Always remember three rules for publication figures: (1) t-SNE/UMAP axes have no feature loadings or explained variance, (2) cluster sizes and inter-cluster gaps in a 2D t-SNE plot are not faithful Euclidean distances, and (3) always perform clustering and statistical tests in the standardized original (or PCA) space, using t-SNE/UMAP strictly for visual intuition.

25.3.9 Break It On Purpose! (Unsupervised Error Anatomy)

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #1 — ValueError (Silhouette Score With Only 1 Cluster) 
all_one_cluster = np.zeros(len(X_scaled), dtype=int)
silhouette_score(X_scaled, all_one_cluster)

Python’s Traceback:

ValueError: Number of labels is 1.
Valid values are 2 to n_samples - 1 (inclusive)

Diagnosis: The silhouette formula requires a nearest other cluster distance \(b(i)\). When every sample has the same label (common with \(k = 1\), or when DBSCAN labels every point as -1 noise or collapses everything into 1 giant blob), \(b(i)\) is undefined.
Fix: Always sweep \(k \ge 2\) and guard DBSCAN silhouette calls by checking len(set(labels) - {-1}) >= 2 before scoring.

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #2 — ValueError (PCA n_components Exceeds Matrix Rank) 
PCA(n_components=12).fit(X_scaled)

Python’s Traceback:

ValueError: n_components=12 must be between 0 and
min(n_samples, n_features)=7 with svd_solver='covariance_eigh'

Diagnosis: A matrix with \(n = 120\) samples and \(p = 7\) features has at most \(\min(n, p) = 7\) orthogonal principal components. Requesting n_components=12 exceeds the mathematical rank limit.
Fix: Cap n_components <= min(X.shape)—or pass a float in (0, 1) (like PCA(n_components=0.85)) or compute k from PCA().fit(X_scaled) dynamically.

ImportantBreak It On Purpose #3 — ValueError / InvalidParameterError (Negative DBSCAN eps) 
DBSCAN(eps=-0.5, min_samples=5).fit(X_scaled)

Python’s Traceback:

InvalidParameterError: The 'eps' parameter of DBSCAN must be a float
in the range (0.0, inf). Got -0.5 instead.

Diagnosis: eps defines a Euclidean neighborhood radius, which must be strictly positive (eps > 0). In Scikit-Learn, InvalidParameterError is a subclass of ValueError raised during estimator parameter validation.
Fix: Ensure eps > 0 (typically chosen via a \(k\)-nearest-neighbor distance “knee” plot in standardized space, such as eps=1.8 on 7 standardized features).

def run_clinical_phenotyping_pipeline(
    raw_df, feat_cols, k=3, var_target=0.85, eps=1.8, seed=42
):
    """Scale vitals, audit PCA compression, and compare clusterers."""
    X = raw_df[feat_cols].to_numpy(dtype=float)
    X_sc = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X)

    # 1. PCA compression & reconstruction MSE audit
    full_pca = PCA().fit(X_sc)
    cum = np.cumsum(full_pca.explained_variance_ratio_)
    n_pcs = int(np.argmax(cum >= var_target) + 1)
    pca_k = PCA(n_components=n_pcs)
    X_hat = pca_k.inverse_transform(pca_k.fit_transform(X_sc))
    recon_mse = round(float(np.mean((X_sc - X_hat) ** 2)), 5)

    # 2. Compare K-Means vs. Ward Agglomerative at k clusters
    km_lbl = KMeans(
        n_clusters=k, n_init=10, random_state=seed
    ).fit_predict(X_sc)
    ward_lbl = AgglomerativeClustering(
        n_clusters=k, linkage="ward"
    ).fit_predict(X_sc)

    # 3. Flag density anomalies via DBSCAN
    db_lbl = DBSCAN(eps=eps, min_samples=5).fit_predict(X_sc)
    return {
        "n_pcs": n_pcs,
        "cum_variance": round(float(cum[n_pcs - 1]), 4),
        "recon_mse": recon_mse,
        "kmeans_sil": round(float(silhouette_score(X_sc, km_lbl)), 4),
        "ward_sil": round(float(silhouette_score(X_sc, ward_lbl)), 4),
        "dbscan_noise": int(np.sum(db_lbl == -1)),
    }

audit = run_clinical_phenotyping_pipeline(clinic, feat_cols)
for key, val in audit.items():

25.8.1 A. Concept & Execution Trace Test Key

  1. Roughly 99.99% (\(10{,}000 / 10{,}001 \approx 99.99\%\)) of squared Euclidean distance comes from Feature A, completely drowning out Feature B until both are standardized.
  2. Inertia strictly decreases as \(k\) grows; at \(k = n\), every point sits at its own centroid so inertia equals 0.0—a useless overfitted partition!
  3. \(s(i) = (1.2 - 1.8) / \max(1.8, 1.2) = -0.6 / 1.8 = \mathbf{-0.3333}\). A negative silhouette means point \(i\) is closer on average to a neighboring cluster than to its own cluster (likely misassigned).
  4. K-Means optimizes a non-convex objective with multiple local minima. One random draw can trap centroids in a higher-inertia local basin (549.53), whereas n_init=10 with k-means++ explores 10 smart starts and selects the lowest-inertia basin (514.06).
  5. No—Ward agglomerative clustering is 100% deterministic because it merges pairs strictly by minimum variance increase from the full pairwise distance matrix (no random centroid seeding).
  6. True n_clusters = 2 (labels 0 and 1) and n_noise = 3 (the three -1 entries). Calling len(set(labels)) returns 3, erroneously counting -1 noise as a third cluster.
  7. Without mean-centering, the first singular vector points from the origin (0, 0, 0) straight toward the data cloud’s mean vector [50, 120, 37], inflating PC1’s variance ratio instead of capturing covariance around the center.
  8. Squared singular values are \([36, 9, 1]\), summing to \(46\). Therefore: \(\text{EVR}_1 = 36/46 \approx \mathbf{0.7826}\), \(\text{EVR}_2 = 9/46 \approx \mathbf{0.1957}\), and \(\text{EVR}_3 = 1/46 \approx \mathbf{0.0217}\).
  9. On standardized features (unit variance per column), reconstruction MSE equals \(1 - 0.8200 = \mathbf{0.18000}\)!
  10. First, t-SNE distorts cluster sizes and global distances to equalize local densities—visual spread on a t-SNE plot does not measure biological variance. Second, running K-Means on 2D t-SNE axes clusters a distorted nonlinear visualization rather than the true standardized feature or PCA space.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans, AgglomerativeClustering, DBSCAN
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score

# To-Do 25.1
def kmeans_from_scratch(X, k, max_iter=50, seed=42):
    """Run pure-NumPy K-Means and return (labels, centroids, inertia)."""
    X_arr = np.asarray(X, dtype=float)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    init_idx = rng.choice(len(X_arr), size=k, replace=False)
    centroids = X_arr[init_idx].copy()
    for _ in range(max_iter):
        dists = ((X_arr[:, None, :] - centroids[None, :, :]) ** 2)
        labels = np.argmin(dists.sum(axis=2), axis=1).astype(int)
        new_c = centroids.copy()
        for cid in range(k):
            mask = labels == cid
            if np.any(mask):
                new_c[cid] = X_arr[mask].mean(axis=0)
        if np.allclose(new_c, centroids):
            centroids = new_c
            break
        centroids = new_c
    dists = ((X_arr[:, None, :] - centroids[None, :, :]) ** 2)
    labels = np.argmin(dists.sum(axis=2), axis=1).astype(int)
    inertia = float(np.sum((X_arr - centroids[labels]) ** 2))
    return labels, centroids, inertia

# To-Do 25.2
def evaluate_k_range(X_scaled, k_values, seed=42):
    """Sweep k_values with KMeans and return inertia/silhouette table."""
    rows = []
    for k in k_values:
        km = KMeans(n_clusters=k, n_init=10, random_state=seed)
        labels = km.fit_predict(X_scaled)
        sil = silhouette_score(X_scaled, labels)
        rows.append({
            "k": int(k),
            "inertia": round(float(km.inertia_), 4),
            "silhouette": round(float(sil), 4),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

# To-Do 25.3
def fit_agglomerative_ward(X_scaled, n_clusters=3):
    """Fit Ward agglomerative clustering and summarize silhouette/sizes."""
    model = AgglomerativeClustering(
        n_clusters=n_clusters, linkage="ward"
    )
    labels = model.fit_predict(X_scaled)
    unique, counts = np.unique(labels, return_counts=True)
    sizes = {int(c): int(n) for c, n in zip(unique, counts)}
    sil = round(float(silhouette_score(X_scaled, labels)), 4)
    return {
        "labels": labels,
        "silhouette": sil,
        "cluster_sizes": sizes,
    }

# To-Do 25.4
def detect_dbscan_anomalies(X_scaled, eps=0.8, min_samples=5):
    """Run DBSCAN and separate dense cluster count from -1 noise count."""
    model = DBSCAN(eps=eps, min_samples=min_samples)
    labels = model.fit_predict(X_scaled)
    n_clusters = int(len(set(labels.tolist()) - {-1}))
    n_noise = int(np.sum(labels == -1))
    return {
        "n_clusters": n_clusters,
        "n_noise": n_noise,
        "labels": labels,
    }

# To-Do 25.5
def pca_from_scratch_svd(X, n_components=2):
    """Center X, run SVD, and return (X_projected, explained_var_ratio)."""
    X_arr = np.asarray(X, dtype=float)
    X_c = X_arr - X_arr.mean(axis=0)
    _, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(X_c, full_matrices=False)
    X_proj = X_c @ Vt[:n_components].T
    evr = (S ** 2) / np.sum(S ** 2)
    return X_proj, evr[:n_components]

# To-Do 25.6
def components_for_variance_target(X_scaled, target_var=0.90):
    """Return smallest int k reaching target_var cumulative variance."""
    pca = PCA().fit(X_scaled)
    cum_var = np.cumsum(pca.explained_variance_ratio_)
    return int(np.argmax(cum_var >= target_var) + 1)

def district_risk_phenotyping(clinic_df, n_clusters=3, seed=42):
    """Aggregate district triage profiles, scale, and cluster."""
    agg = clinic_df.groupby("district", as_index=False).agg(
        mean_temp=("temp_c", "mean"),
        mean_sbp=("systolic_bp", "mean"),
        mean_hb=("hemoglobin_g_dl", "mean"),
        urgent_rate=(
            "triage_priority", lambda s: (s != "Routine").mean()
        ),
    )
    cols = ["mean_temp", "mean_sbp", "mean_hb", "urgent_rate"]

    agg["cluster"] = labels

        "district_clusters": agg,
        "silhouette": sil,
    }

# Exam Task 25.3 (35 pts)
def pca_clinical_compression_audit(X, variance_threshold=0.85):
    """Scale X, pick PCs for variance_threshold, and audit recon MSE."""
    X_sc = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X)
    full_pca = PCA().fit(X_sc)
    cum = np.cumsum(full_pca.explained_variance_ratio_)
    k = int(np.argmax(cum >= variance_threshold) + 1)
    pca_k = PCA(n_components=k)
    Z = pca_k.fit_transform(X_sc)
    X_hat = pca_k.inverse_transform(Z)
    cum_var = round(float(np.sum(pca_k.explained_variance_ratio_)), 4)
    top_idx = int(np.argmax(np.abs(pca_k.components_[0])))
    mse = round(float(np.mean((X_sc - X_hat) ** 2)), 5)
    return {
        "n_components": k,
        "cumulative_variance": cum_var,
        "top_pc1_feature_idx": top_idx,
        "reconstruction_mse": mse,
    }

25.8.4 D. Debugging Post-Mortem: Top 3 Chapter 25 Failure Modes

  1. Running Euclidean Clustering or PCA on Unscaled Units: Leaving raw features unstandardized lets large-unit variables (age_years variance 451.44 vs. temp_c variance 0.60) monopolize both cluster boundaries and PC1 loadings. Always apply StandardScaler() before K-Means, Ward, DBSCAN, or PCA unless all features share identical physical units.
  2. Miscounting DBSCAN -1 Noise or Crashing silhouette_score: Forgetting - {-1} turns DBSCAN outliers into a phantom cluster, and calling silhouette_score when only one cluster exists immediately raises ValueError. Always separate noise counts first and verify at least two clusters exist before scoring.
  3. Forgetting Mean-Centering or np.abs(...) in PCA Analysis: Running np.linalg.svd without subtracting column means points PC1 toward the global mean rather than maximum variance, while taking np.argmax(pca.components_[0]) without np.abs(...) misses strong negative loadings because PCA eigenvector signs are arbitrary.
